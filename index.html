Abstract

A SKOS concept scheme of the characters of the Mahabharata, the Sanskrit epic of the dynastic war between the Pandavas and the Kauravas for the throne of Hastinapura. Each character is a skos:Concept placed under the lineage, royal house, order or class of being the epic gives it. Collections gather characters across that hierarchy: the two sides of the war, birth order, the survivors, the narrative frame. Kinship and narrative relations between characters use sub-properties of skos:related from the companion schema. Labels are given in English, in Sanskrit in Devanagari (sa-Deva) and in IAST transliteration (sa-Latn). Epithets and patronymics are alternative labels; common spellings are hidden labels. Every character with an English Wikipedia article is mapped to its Wikidata item and DBpedia resource.

1. Introduction

The Mahabharata is the Sanskrit epic of the dynastic war between the Pandavas and the Kauravas for the throne of Hastinapura. This scheme is a knowledge organisation system for its characters: the people, gods and other beings who act or are named in it. It is written in SKOS [SKOS-REFERENCE] and published as RDF 1.1 Turtle [TURTLE].

Every character is a skos:Concept placed under the lineage, royal house, order or class of being the epic gives it. Kinship and narrative ties between characters (parent, spouse, teacher, killer, charioteer, rebirth, curse) are sub-properties of skos:related, declared in a small extension schema. Labels are given in English, in Sanskrit in Devanagari script and in IAST transliteration, with epithets as alternative labels and common spellings as hidden labels. Every character with an English Wikipedia article is mapped to its Wikidata item and DBpedia resource. A SHACL [SHACL] shapes file states the SKOS integrity conditions and the conventions of the scheme.

The content follows the Wikipedia article Mahabharata [WIKIPEDIA] and the character articles it links to. The scheme holds 111 characters under 37 groupings, with 11 collections that cut across the hierarchy.

1.1 Scope and sources

The scheme covers the characters named in the synopsis of the Wikipedia article and the principal figures of the Kurukshetra war: 111 characters. The epic names many hundreds more (the Kauravas alone are a hundred brothers); the Wikipedia list of characters [WIKIPEDIA-LIST] is the natural source for additions. In scope are the characters of the Mahabharata proper, including its frame story and the deities who act in it. Out of scope are characters who appear only in the Ramopakhyana retelling of the Ramayana, and figures from regional retellings.

Parentage, deaths and the days on which warriors fell follow the Wikipedia articles, which follow the Critical Edition of the Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute and note the variants. Where recensions differ, the commonly cited reading is used. Devanagari and IAST forms follow standard dictionary practice. The descriptions paraphrase the articles, which are published under CC BY-SA 4.0; the same licence applies to the scheme (Sources, licence and citation).

1.3 How to read this document

The sections up to Collections describe the scheme; the cross-reference sections hold one entry per term. An entry is a table headed by the kind of term, its English label and its prefixed name. The colour of the band says what kind of term it is:

  • Scheme
  • Class
  • Property
  • Grouping
  • Character
  • Collection
  • Shape

Names. Terms are written with the prefixes of the namespace table: mbh:arjuna is the concept Arjuna, mbhx:childOf a property of the schema. Every prefixed name in this document links to the entry of its term; a term of another vocabulary links to its own IRI.

IRIs resolve here. The IRI of a term is the first row of its entry. The scheme uses hash IRIs built on three namespace documents served beside this page: /characters for concepts and collections, /schema for classes and properties and /shapes for the SHACL shapes. Each lists its terms and forwards a browser to the entry here that the fragment names, so https://mahabharata.tanay.uk/characters#arjuna opens the entry for Arjuna. The site does not negotiate content: the Turtle is at fixed addresses (Access and use).

Relations are stated once. The data writes each relation in the direction its property names: mbhx:childOf on the child, mbhx:killedBy on the one killed, mbhx:studentOf on the student. An entry shows those statements under the label of the property, and the other direction under the label of the declared inverse (parent of, killed, teacher of), marked inferred, computed from the statements in the data. Symmetric relations (spouse of, sibling of, friend of, rival of) are stated once per pair and shown on both.

Languages. Labels carry language tags [BCP47]: en English, sa-Deva Sanskrit in Devanagari script, sa-Latn Sanskrit in IAST transliteration.

Counts. Every number in this document is computed from the data when the page is generated. The count after a grouping is the number of characters placed under it or under its narrower groupings; a character under two groupings is counted under both.

2. The scheme at a glance

The scheme has two kinds of concept. A mbhx:Character is a person, deity or other being that acts or is named in the epic. A mbhx:Grouping is a lineage, generation, royal house, order or class of being under which characters are placed. Both are subclasses of skos:Concept, and they are disjoint.

skos:broader runs from a character to its groupings and from a grouping to a wider grouping. A character is never broader than another character: the hierarchy is a classification, not a genealogy. Family ties are expressed as relations instead, so skos:broader+ always means belongs to and mbhx:childOf+ always means descends from.

2.2 Properties

Relations between characters are sub-properties of skos:related, declared in the extension schema (mb:schema). Specialising SKOS this way is the pattern of the SKOS Primer, section 4.7 [SKOS-PRIMER]: every mbhx:killedBy statement is also a skos:related statement for a tool that knows only SKOS, and a tool that knows the schema gets the finer meaning. Each relation is stated once, in the direction the property names; the inverse is declared with owl:inverseOf and left to inference. 10. Validation

The shapes in shapes.ttl (293 triples, 10 node shapes) state what the data must satisfy, in SHACL [SHACL]. scripts/validate.py runs them with pySHACL under RDFS inference, so that sub-properties and subclasses count, then checks that every mbh: IRI used in the data is also declared. Continuous integration runs the validator on every push and fails on any violation.

pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/validate.py

The shapes have two layers:

LayerWhat is checked
SKOS integrity conditions (S9, S13, S14, S19, S27, S36, S37, S46 of the SKOS Reference)At most one prefLabel per language; prefLabel, altLabel and hiddenLabel pairwise disjoint; a concept is neither a scheme nor a collection; broader points at concepts; related is disjoint with the transitive hierarchy; an ordered collection has exactly one well-formed member list that agrees with skos:member; exactMatch is disjoint with broadMatch and relatedMatch.
Conventions of this schemeEvery concept has an English preferred label and definition and is in the scheme; it is a top concept or has a broader concept, never both; a character sits under groupings only and is never a top concept; a grouping has narrower concepts and carries no character relations; relations connect characters to characters, in one direction only, symmetric ones once per pair, with no self-relations and no parent-child cycles; mapping IRIs match the Wikidata, DBpedia and Wikipedia patterns; every character has an external anchor; fellOnWarDay is an integer from 1 to 18.

11. Access and use

The scheme is one RDF 1.1 Turtle file, mahabharata_characters.ttl (2,569 triples); the shapes are shapes.ttl. Both are served beside this page and declared as rel="alternate" links in its head. The site does not negotiate content: a request for a namespace IRI returns HTML, and the Turtle is fetched from these two addresses. The repository's scripts/export.py writes RDF/XML, JSON-LD and N-Triples copies for tools that want them.

The file is plain Turtle, so it opens in Protégé, TopBraid Composer, Skosmos (through a SPARQL endpoint such as Fuseki) and any RDF library. With Apache Jena, riot --validate mahabharata_characters.ttl checks the syntax and arq --data=mahabharata_characters.ttl --query=q.rq runs a query. With rdflib:

from rdflib import Graph, Namespace
from rdflib.collection import Collection
from rdflib.namespace import SKOS

g = Graph().parse("https://mahabharata.tanay.uk/mahabharata_characters.ttl")
MBH = Namespace("https://mahabharata.tanay.uk/characters#")
MBHX = Namespace("https://mahabharata.tanay.uk/schema#")

def label(node, lang="en"):
    return next(str(l) for l in g.objects(node, SKOS.prefLabel) if l.language == lang)

# Relations are stated in one direction: killedBy on the one who was killed.
print(sorted(label(c) for c in g.subjects(MBHX.killedBy, MBH.bhima)))

# Walk up the hierarchy.
print([label(b) for b in g.transitive_objects(MBH.draupadi, SKOS.broader) if b != MBH.draupadi])

# Read an ordered collection in order.
head = g.value(MBH["coll-kaurava-commanders"], SKOS.memberList)
print([label(m) for m in Collection(g, head)])
